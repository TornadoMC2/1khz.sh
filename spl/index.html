planning numbers

Level over distance

A point source in free field loses 6 dB every time the distance doubles. That is the inverse square law, and it is the number to plan with — but it is also the number a real venue will argue with. Indoors, once you are past critical distance the reverberant field stops the level falling at all. A line array in its near field loses closer to 3 dB per doubling, which is the entire point of a line array. Treat the answer as the worst case for a single box and expect reality to be kinder.

Sensitivity and amplifier power

Speaker sensitivity is the SPL produced by one watt at one metre, and it is the most leveraged number in the whole chain: 3 dB more sensitivity is worth as much as doubling your amplifier. Going the other way is brutal arithmetic — every extra 3 dB doubles the power required, so +10 dB needs ten times the amp. This is why the honest answer to "can we just turn it up" is usually more boxes rather than a bigger amplifier.

Headroom is not optional. Programme material has a crest factor of maybe 10–20 dB between average and peak, so an amplifier sized for the average level will clip on every transient. Size for the peaks.

Speaker cable

Speaker cable and the driver form a voltage divider, so the loss depends on the ratio of cable resistance to load impedance — which means low-impedance loads punish thin cable far harder. A long run of 16 AWG into a 2 Ω sub load can throw away a serious fraction of the amplifier's output as heat in the copper. Keeping the loss under about 0.5 dB is the usual rule, and going up a gauge or two is much cheaper than going up an amplifier.
