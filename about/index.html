About This Site

1K-INFO · the honest version

1kHz.sh is a signal generator and a handful of audio calculators, built for the kind of work that happens standing up: load-in, soundcheck, ringing out a room, arguing about a delay tower. It is meant to be the thing you can pull up on a phone with one bar of venue Wi-Fi and trust the answer from.

It is free, it has no accounts, it shows no ads, and it collects nothing. That is easy to write on a webpage and worth nothing unless you can check it, so the rest of this page is mostly about how to check it.

It is built and maintained by one person — Hunter Johanson (@TornadoMC2) — as an open-source project under the MIT licence. Every line of it is on GitHub. The plan is to keep adding tools that are genuinely useful on a gig, and to keep the whole thing loading in well under a second.

Privacy

specific claims, not a policy

There is no backend. Nothing you type into any field on this site is transmitted anywhere — not to me, not to anyone. Every calculation runs as JavaScript inside your own browser, on your own device.

What this site does not do

  • No analytics. No Google Analytics, no Plausible, no Fathom, no self-hosted counter, no pixel, no beacon.
  • No cookies. Not one, of any kind, including "essential" ones. There is nothing to consent to, which is why there is no banner.
  • No local storage. Nothing is written to localStorage, sessionStorage, or IndexedDB. Close the tab and no trace of your session remains.
  • No third-party requests. Zero. The fonts are served from this domain rather than Google Fonts specifically so that loading a page here does not tell Google you did. There are no CDNs, no embeds, no iframes.
  • No accounts, no email, no sign-up. There is nothing to register for.
  • No ads, and no affiliate links. Nothing on this site is placed because someone paid for it.

What is unavoidably visible

Being honest about this matters more than the list above. Static files still have to be served by a web server, and web servers see requests:

  • The site is hosted on Cloudflare, which necessarily receives your IP address, user agent, and the URL you asked for in order to send the page back. That is how HTTP works, and it applies to every website you have ever visited.
  • Because the frequency lives in the hostname, the subdomain you visit is part of that request — loading 440.1khz.sh means "440" appears in the URL, whereas typing 440 into the dial once you are here does not.
  • I do not run analytics on top of that data, and I do not have any interest in it. But I would rather tell you the mechanism than claim a kind of invisibility that no hosted website can honestly offer.

If even that bothers you: install it as an app or load it once and turn off your network. It works completely offline, at which point nothing leaves your device at all. That is not a workaround — it is the intended way to use it at a gig.

Don't Take My Word For It

four checks, about a minute

Every claim on this page is verifiable from the browser you are reading it in. You do not need to trust me, and you shouldn't have to.

  1. Watch the network. Open your browser's developer tools (F12, or Cmd‑Option‑I on a Mac), go to the Network tab, and reload. You will see this page, one stylesheet, a couple of scripts, and the fonts — all from 1khz.sh. Now go use the calculators. Type in every field, drag every fader, run the generator. Nothing new appears. There is nowhere for your data to go.
  2. Pull the plug. Load any page here, then switch on airplane mode and reload it. It still works, because the service worker cached the whole kit on your device. A site that keeps functioning with no network is a site that was not phoning home.
  3. Check the jar. In developer tools, open Application (or Storage). Cookies: empty. Local storage: empty. The only thing there is the offline cache, which holds the site's own files and nothing about you.
  4. Read it. There is no build step and no bundler, so the JavaScript running in your browser is exactly the JavaScript in the repository — not minified, not transpiled, not obfuscated. View source on this page, or read the whole project on GitHub. It is a few hundred lines. The maths is all in one file.

That last point is the one that does the real work. Plenty of sites promise privacy; not many ship source you can read in a sitting and then compare against what your browser actually loaded.

Using It Safely

tones into a PA are not a toy

A signal generator plugged into a real system can hurt your ears and kill drivers faster than music can, because it never stops and it never dips. Sustained tones carry far more energy than programme material at the same meter reading.

  • Output always starts off, and the level starts at −18 dBFS. That is deliberate. Bring gain up from the system, not from this page.
  • High frequencies kill compression drivers. Continuous tones and full-range noise above a few kHz will cook a horn at levels that feel moderate. Keep it short, keep it low.
  • Protect your hearing first. Sustained pure tones are genuinely more damaging than they sound, and they mask their own loudness. If you are checking something at level, wear protection and keep it brief.
  • Check the patch before the power. Know where the output is going before you switch it on. Headphones on a desk are a classic way to discover the level was wrong.
  • The 16 kHz "hearing test" is not a hearing test. Most phone and laptop speakers cannot reproduce it at all. If you hear nothing, the likeliest explanation is the speaker, not your ears. See an audiologist for the real answer.

Accuracy

what these numbers are worth

The formulas are standard and implemented carefully, but every one of them rests on assumptions that a real room will cheerfully violate. Sabine assumes a diffuse field; the SPL maths assumes free field and a point source; the delay maths assumes still air at a uniform temperature. None of those are true at a gig.

So: these are planning numbers, not measurements. They will tell you what to expect and what size of answer is reasonable. They will not tell you what your system is actually doing — only a measurement microphone does that.

Every formula, constant, and assumption is written out in full on the Formulas & Assumptions page, including the places where each one stops being trustworthy. If you find an error, please open an issue — corrections are the most useful contribution anyone can make here.

Open Source

MIT · take it and run

The whole project is on GitHub under the MIT licence: use it, fork it, host your own copy, put it on a laptop in a shop, ship it inside something else. No attribution theatre required beyond keeping the licence file.

There is no framework, no bundler, and no dependencies — it is HTML, one stylesheet, and a handful of ES modules. You can clone it and serve the folder and it just runs. The two bundled webfonts (Barlow Semi Condensed and IBM Plex Mono) are third-party and separately licensed under the SIL Open Font License.

How it stays free

It costs almost nothing to run: static files on a free hosting tier, and a domain. There is no business model, no investor, and nothing to upsell.

There is a Ko-fi link in the footer. It is the only thing on this site that asks you for anything, it is entirely optional, and nothing is gated, degraded, or nagged if you ignore it — which most people should. If anything about this ever changes — sponsorship, affiliate links, anything at all — it will be disclosed on this page rather than quietly rolled out.

Install it

The site is a Progressive Web App. On a phone, use "Add to Home Screen"; on a desktop browser, look for the install icon in the address bar. It then launches like an app, opens instantly, and works with no signal at all — which is the actual reason it exists in this form.
